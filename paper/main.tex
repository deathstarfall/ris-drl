\title{Model-Driven Optimization for Reconfigurable Intelligent
Surfaces: Deep Unfolding versus a Null Result for Direct
Reinforcement Learning}
\author{A.~Agarwal, S.~Khatri, B.~Singh, and S.~Jha%
\thanks{The authors are with the School of Electronics Engineering,
Vellore Institute of Technology, Vellore 632014, India.
Corresponding author: A.~Agarwal. The evaluation artifacts and the
supplementary project decision log are open-source at
\texttt{github.com/deathstarfall/ris-drl}.}}
\maketitle

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
Reconfigurable intelligent surface, deep unfolding, reinforcement
learning, sum-rate maximization, Riemannian optimization, phase
quantization, null result.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\begin{abstract}
We compare three solver families for phase-configuration
optimization of a reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) under a
zero-forcing sum-rate objective with direct-link blockage, on one
frozen 500-channel test set fixed before evaluation: a Riemannian
alternating-optimize (AO) reference, a deep unfolding of
projected-gradient ascent, and six direct reinforcement-learning (RL)
policies spanning SAC, TD3, DDPG, and codebook-DQN at 2--4-bit phase
resolution. Deep unfolding reaches $62.53$~bps/Hz mean sum rate
(SD $0.85$), $2.6\%$ short of the Riemannian AO reference
($64.19\pm0.58$, SD) and $9.35$~bps/Hz above the random baseline
($53.18$; $p=1.3\times10^{-83}$), while deciding in about $1.3$~ms per
channel (median $1.295$, $p95$ $1.312$). No RL policy exceeds random
(deltas $-0.10$ to $+0.04$~bps/Hz, all $p \geq 0.37$), whereas a
200-draw random search attains $57.14\pm0.76$ (SD) over the same
channels. The null is therefore informative: this regime is exploited
by structure-informed optimization, and model-free exploration at the
same budget does not find it. Every value in the paper is emitted from
committed machine-readable artifacts.
\end{abstract}
