\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Under a frozen 500-channel evaluation protocol, direct
reinforcement-learning phase policies for a blocked direct-link RIS
scenario provided no measurable benefit over random phase selection,
across every investigated architecture (continuous and codebook
discretization levels) and sixty independent training runs---thirty
under the published partial observation and thirty under a full-observation
stress test that handed every agent the channel phases it was otherwise
denied. Deep unfolding, by contrast, recovers $97.4\%$ of the
Riemannian-AO reference at $\sim$1.3~ms decision latency, absorbs 2--4-bit
quantization losses gracefully, and degrades coherently under channel
error. The asymmetry between the two epistemologies is not
sample-limited (the 200-draw random oracle sits $8$~bps/Hz above both),
it is not information-limited (the full-observation campaign reproduces
the null), it is \emph{structure}-limited.

\paragraph{Scope.} All claims above are established within one fixed
regime: $N{=}64$ RIS elements, $M{=}8$ base-station antennas, $K{=}4$
downlink users, per-entry circularly symmetric complex Gaussian
(Rayleigh) i.i.d.\ channel coefficients under 3GPP urban-micro path
loss with a $-25$~dB-blocked direct path (zero-forcing sum-rate
objective), and 500 frozen test channels drawn from that generator.
The null result (no architecture exceeding random) is a statement
about \emph{this regime and protocol}; richer geometries, correlated
fading, hardware-quantized RIS ports, and multi-user non-ZF
objectives are natural places where the null could break; within this
regime, the full-observation stress test shows the null is robust to
the strongest information-side objection available.

We offer this not as a verdict against RL writ large, but against an
evaluation culture in which trained-policy comparisons are reported
without frozen test sets, written acceptance rules, or disciplined
selection rules. The three-layer protocol---AO reference,
model-driven learner, model-free challenger---used here is portable to
any emerging-RIS benchmark, and we release the decision log
(the supplementary decision log), the raw per-channel artifacts, and the entire
figure/statistics pipeline so that any claimed RL-vs-unfolding
ordering can, from now on, be checked against committed files rather
than prose.

\paragraph{Future work.} Three ranked directions follow directly from
the reported data: (i) reward shaping and state-augmented
observations that inject gradient-sign structure into the bandit
observation, giving model-free learners the same structure unfolding
consumes at design time; (ii) sub-millisecond forward-only inference
on hardware targets versus the measured $8.2$~ms, quantifying latency
on real silicon rather than software proxies; and (iii) extension to
multi-RIS and multi-user scaling, where the AO-reference gap is expected
to widen the way it did for the single-RIS hard case here.
