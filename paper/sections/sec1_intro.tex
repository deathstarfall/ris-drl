\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces reshape the wireless propagation
environment by reflecting incident waves under a programmable phase
profile~\cite{huang2019reconfigurable,wu2019towards}, turning the
passive radio scene into an optimizable multiplier. Achieving that
potential, however, requires solving a non-convex phase
optimization problem per coherence block under real deployments that
must obey latency budgets, quantization limits, and imperfect channel
state information~\cite{wu2020intelligent}.

Two solution philosophies currently dominate the literature. The
\emph{model-driven} family embeds the sum-rate objective and its
gradients explicitly into the solver: alternating-optimize (AO)
procedures descend a Riemannian manifold
structure~\cite{absil2008optimization,zhang2021manifold}, while
\emph{deep unfolding} \cite{shlezinger2023model} converts iterations of
a projected-gradient method into layers of a fixed-depth neural
network, regaining learned step sizes while keeping the physics
visible---a lineage that began with AMP-inspired networks
\cite{borgerding2017amp} and has since been applied to RIS
beamforming directly \cite{liu2022deepunfolding}. The \emph{model-free} family treats phase selection as a
decision problem and trains reinforcement-learning
agents~\cite{mnih2015human,lillicrap2015continuous,fujimoto2018addressing,haarnoja2018soft}
end-to-end from reward. Learning-designed signal focusing predates the
current RL wave~\cite{huang2019indoor}; DRL-based phase configuration
was analyzed by Huang et al.~\cite{huang2020risdrl}; a recent
Proc.~IEEE overview maps the learning-for-RIS landscape
\cite{alexandropoulos2022pervasive}; and sum-rate objectives with
discrete phases are commonly attacked by alternating or manifold
methods~\cite{guo2020weighted,zhang2021manifold}. Recent single-family
studies extend DRL phase control into dedicated regimes such as
mmWave high-speed-rail links~\cite{xu2022when} and 6G multi-user NLOS
deployments~\cite{aasa2026deep}, robust-DRL variants handle RIS
energy-harvesting terminals under their own robustness
simulations~\cite{peng2023energy}, hybrid learning accelerates IRS
beamforming~\cite{sivasankar2025fast}, ML-based beamforming overviews
consolidate the landscape~\cite{ahmadinejad2025beamforming}, DRL
synthesizes discrete-phase beam patterns for mmWave
surfaces~\cite{nishad2026deep}, and RIS channel acquisition---the
motivation for partial-observation stacks---is addressed by
LMMSE-to-DL and super-resolution estimation
frameworks~\cite{kundu2021channel,shen2023deep}. These works all
evaluate their own simulators in their own setups, which leaves the

\subsection{Contributions}
\label{sec:contrib}

This paper contributes a protocol-governed empirical comparison of
these philosophies, built around a frozen test protocol rather than
best-effort best-run reporting:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Frozen evaluation protocol.} A fixed channel-generation
seed split (train/test/generalize)
fixes all 500 evaluation channels before any policy interacts with
them; every method---AO reference, unfolding, and all six RL agents---is
evaluated through one shared scorekeeper on identical hardware and an
identical zero-forcing sum-rate convention (\S\ref{sec:intro}).
\item \textbf{A Riemannian AO reference.} A manifold-constrained
alternating optimizer reaches $64.19$~bps/Hz mean sum rate
($1.207 \times$ the random baseline) on the full frozen test set,
establishing the headroom available to a learned method (the problem
is non-convex, so this is a strong reference solution reached under a
fixed 200-iteration stopping rule, not a provable upper bound)
(\S\ref{sec:results-ao}).
\item \textbf{Deep unfolding within $2.6\%$ of the reference.} An
$L{=}6$ unfolded projected-gradient network reaches $62.53$~bps/Hz
($p=1.3\times10^{-83}$ over random), trained under an audited
train/validation protocol with a single frozen-test evaluation, with
inference latency of $1.3$~ms (median) per decision and an absorbed
quantization sweep at 2/3/4-bit phase codebooks (\S\ref{sec:results-ao}).
\item \textbf{A null result for direct DRL policies.} Six
architectures $\times$ five seeds of SAC/TD3/DDPG/coded-codebook
DQN trains show no statistically significant positive mean gain over
random on the frozen
test set ($\Delta \in [-0.10,+0.04]$ bps/Hz, all $p \geq 0.37$,
\S\ref{sec:results-drl}); a follow-up full-observation campaign that
retrains all thirty checkpoints with complete channel-phase access
reproduces the null to within $+0.03$~bps/Hz of the floor, while a
no-learning closed-form full-CSI policy reaches $+3.77$ over the floor;
under these budgets and default hyperparameters the DRL configurations
fail to learn rather than fail for lack of information. The
study reports this outcome rather than
selecting training-run endpoints, and contrasts it against the
$+3.97$~bps/Hz obtainable from a simple 200-draw random oracle,
isolating \emph{learned structure}---not sample count, not information
access---as the
difference-maker.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Reproducibility and the role of a decision log}
\label{sec:repro}

Every numeric claim in this paper is emitted from committed JSON
artifacts by a figure pipeline that accepts no hand-typed values
(the pipeline accepts no hand-typed values; see the supplementary
decision log), and every experimental-convention change en route is
recorded as a numbered entry with date and owner. In particular, changes that materially weakened the original
claim set---replacement of an interference-limited channel model, opening of the
direct link to $-25$~dB blockage so that RIS contribution dominates
(G1 sensitivity check $3.75$~bps/Hz/flip), and re-casting the RL
formulation as a $T{=}1$ contextual bandit---are logged \emph{as corrections}, not as
silent tuning. We argue that such a log is the minimum honest
protocol for RL-vs-optimization comparisons, where training-budget,
checkpoint-selection, and test-set-consumption asymmetries otherwise
manufacture spurious ordering.
